  - That is the predicted direction. But it is each column's most extreme cell, picked after the fact, against standard errors of 13–18 bp. Nothing here contradicts the mechanism, and nothing confirms it.

**2 · The spans trace the textbook decay path, and the last step cannot be read.**
- d runs +6.2 → +2.3 → −2.8%/yr. The 2014–2023 figure is 63% below 2005–2013 (my arithmetic), close to the 58% average post-publication decline in McLean & Pontiff.
- 2024-01 → 2026-07 is 31 windows. Its standard error is about 4.5 points a year, implied by the printed z of −0.61. That cannot separate "still at the 2014–2023 size" (1.1 SE away) from "gone".
- The full-sample +3.3%/yr leans on the first span, which is in-sample. My judgement is that the forward value lies below the 2014–2023 figure and may be zero.

**3 · FOMC: yes, plausibly a large part, in the years that can be checked.**
- In the 160 dated windows, the 46 with a scheduled statement (28.7%) supplied about 58% of the cycle excess (0.287 × 68.8 against 0.713 × 20.2 bp; my arithmetic).
- The +48.6 bp gap is about one standard error (about 46 bp, my estimate from TE 9.04% over 46 and 114 cycles). It is the same order as the published pre-FOMC drift: about 49 bp (Lucca & Moench, via VoxEU), which adds about 33 bp to a cycle at 2×.
- So part of the 2005–2013 "month-end" excess may be the announcement premium riding on late-month meetings. That premium's reported death after 2015 (Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert) would then account for some of the decay.
- This split cannot test that idea, because 2017–2024 is undated. Without FOMC windows, about 20 bp a cycle remains, roughly 2.4%/yr, in the dated years.
- By contrast, rsi_dip holds on only 5.2% of the 2× sessions, so the effect is not the reversal premium measured again.

**4 · For Daniel.**
The month-end flow is probably real, but it has shrunk below what this market's noise lets us certify.
- **What the backtest showed.** Over 2005–2026, extra exposure placed in the month-end window beat the same exposure spread evenly by 3.8 points a year. Random placement within each month matched that only rarely (p = 0.011).
- **Why that is not enough.** It reached z = 1.7, against a bar of 3.03 set by 39 earlier searches. It also shrank from +6.2%/yr to +2.3% to −2.8% (2024–26), and part of it may have been an FOMC premium that has since disappeared. The verdict is PARTIAL — do not trade.
- **How to hold SPY.** Nothing here changes how he holds it. Hold through every night and every month-end, and neither lever up nor step out around the turn of the month. Holding only the window at 1× earned 8.0%/yr, against 10.6% for buy-and-hold.
- **Where the overlay's lead came from.** It beat buy-and-hold by 5.6 points a year. About 1.8 of those points came simply from holding more stock, which is a risk choice, not an edge. The rest is the timing that did not clear the bar.
- **Testing it again.** The settlement shift and the FOMC overlap are hypotheses for a new pre-registration, tested only on month-ends after 2026-08. At 12 windows a year and a 9% tracking error, reaching t = 2 on a 2–3%/yr effect takes roughly 35–80 years (my arithmetic). He should not wait for that test, or trade in anticipation of it.
