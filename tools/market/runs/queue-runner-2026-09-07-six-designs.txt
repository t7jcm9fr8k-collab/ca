## why the Dore plate cannot print: the halftone measurement
The Paradise Lost plate is a continuous tonal wash, not linework. Measured on
2026-09-09 in the same container: a clustered-dot screen (the thing a press
would use) loses this much of its ink area under the 3 px erosion qc.py
applies, at three tones, before any dilation:

  pitch   8 px  (38 lpi)   80: 100%   128: 100%   190:  99%
  pitch  12 px  (25 lpi)   80: 100%   128:  97%   190:  86%
  pitch  16 px  (19 lpi)   80:  97%   128:  89%   190:  72%
  pitch  20 px  (15 lpi)   80:  89%   128:  76%   190:  64%
  pitch  26 px  (12 lpi)   80:  75%   128:  63%   190:  51%
  pitch  32 px  ( 9 lpi)   80:  65%   128:  53%   190:  44%
  pitch  40 px  ( 8 lpi)   80:  52%   128:  43%   190:  37%

No pitch clears the 35% ceiling. Dilating the screen does clear it, and fills
the tone in: pitch 16 + thicken 4 gives 34% loss at tone 80 but 94% ink
coverage, over the 55% ceiling — a slab again. So the screen was written,
measured, and removed rather than shipped: a DTG printer rasterises tone
itself, so pre-screening the file is a screen-print step this pipeline does
not take, and the honest reading is that a tonal wash has no marks that
survive at 15 inches on fabric. Dore's Descent needs a line-engraved plate.
